the one idea

Model the anatomy. The syndrome falls out.

Weber, Wallenberg, Brown-Séquard — none of these are written down anywhere in the engine. They emerge from which anatomical structures share a lesion site, and are only named afterward by a lookup. Delete the name table and the engine still localises; it just returns anatomy instead of an eponym.

The engine only ever knows anatomy and arithmetic: which structure produces which sign on which side, and how well a candidate lesion's predicted signs match the ones the patient actually has.

No rule ever says if (hasX && hasY) return "Weber". That would be storing a brain. Instead X and Y are structures that happen to share one blood supply — so they fail together, and the pattern is derived for every possible lesion, every time.

the data model — how the pieces relate

Four nouns, and a syndrome for free

The whole model is four kinds of thing. Follow one sign — spinothalamic loss — down the ladder and watch a named syndrome assemble itself from parts that were never told about each other.

Finding findings.js

One thing you can observe at the bedside — a symptom or sign. It carries a default crossing (does its pathway cross the midline?) and is later marked with the body side it appears on.

e.g.spinothalamic pain & temperature loss · crosses = true
Structure structures.js

One tract or nucleus. It produces exactly one finding, sits at a (level, part) — its place in the neuraxis and its vascular/anatomical zone — and may override the crossing where the same pathway crosses differently.

e.g.stt_cord → spinothalamic at cord · anterior · crosses in the cord → contralateral
Site sites.js

A lesion — one zone on one side. It is derived, never hand-listed: it simply gathers every structure that shares its (level, part, side). Bigger lesions come from composers that union parts (a hemicord, a bilateral cord, a foramen, a plexus trunk).

e.g.left_cord_hemi = anterior ∪ posterior on the left → gathers cst_cord_arm cst_cord_leg stt_cord dc_cord …
Syndrome emergent · syndromes.js only names it

Not a thing you store — a thing that happens. When the structures at a site fire together, their signs co-occur in a pattern. A thin phonebook keyed by the emergent site attaches the eponym, differentials and red flags.

emerges →ipsilateral weakness + contralateral pain/temp + ipsilateral vibration = Brown-Séquard — because those three tracts share the hemicord

how solve() works — the inverse problem

Try every lesion. Keep the one that fits.

Localisation is the model run backwards: the patient's findings are given, the lesion is unknown. So the engine simulates every candidate lesion forwards, then scores which one best reproduces what the patient has.

1

Generate every candidate lesion

candidateSites() · sites.js

Every primitive site plus every composite (hemi, bilateral, midline, foramen-union, plexus, vascular territory). All derived from the structure tables — a few hundred candidates.

2

Simulate each one — forwards

expectedFindings(site) · forward.js

Predict the signs a lesion there would cause, each tagged with a body side. Crossing decides ipsi vs contra; a midline lesion emits both sides; a sensory level can gate a sign on or off; cortical dominance gates the language-side signs.

3

Score the fit against the patient

scoreSite() · score.js

Reward matches — localising signs count triple. Penalise findings the site can't explain, and penalise over-prediction (signs it predicts but the patient lacks). Parsimony is the whole game: the tightest, most complete fit wins.

4

Rank — or, if needed, a minimal set

rankSingle() · minimalSet() · inverse.js

Sort for the single best explanation. If no one lesion covers all the localising signs, find the smallest set of lesions that does — the multifocal hypothesis, derived rather than dropped in as a fallback.

Orthogonal axes — they annotate, they never decide

Two coordinates run across the cross-sectional pattern and only refine the winner. The sensory level (levels.js, C2–S5) says where along the cord; the axon-length axis (nerveLength.js) says how far a length-dependent neuropathy has climbed. The cross-section says which syndrome; these say where.

what sits on top — one lesion is only the first answer

Where, why, what, and what next

Localisation is the foundation, not the product. Three layers are built on it, and each keeps content apart from logic — a clinician reviews any one of them by opening a single file that imports nothing from the interface.

Why

the reasoning, composed — not stored

  • Ten pathways modelled, each with its course and the point at which it crosses
  • A narrative assembled from the anatomy, plus why not elsewhere — the sign each rival site would have added
  • A neuraxis diagram derived from the same tables

What

the differential at that place

  • The surgical sieve, filtered by the tempo the examiner entered
  • A discriminating feature on nearly every cause, and a bedside sign to confirm it
  • A tempo mismatch demotes a cause — it never deletes it

What next

educational prompts, never a directive

  • Four tiers — immediate, first-line, confirmatory, monitoring
  • Choose a disease and the lower tiers narrow to it: the tiers divide by when you know the cause
  • Nothing flagged must-not-miss can render as routine

And when one lesion is not enough

If the picture genuinely needs two places, the engine first tries to talk you out of it — naming any single sign that, dropped, would collapse the case back to one lesion. Only then does it ask the real question: what one disease attacks both of these places? That answer comes from the tissue a disease targets rather than from a stored list of site pairs — vasculitis crosses the central-to-peripheral boundary because vessels are on both sides of it, and metastases do not, because parenchyma is not. Anatomy filters; tempo and course only demote.

the code, in three layers

Where each thing lives

Data flows one way — model → engine → data. You add coverage by editing the model tables; the engine almost never changes.

src/model/

the anatomy — edit these to add coverage

  • findings.js — the vocabulary + crossing defaults
  • structures.js — one structure → one finding, placed & crossed
  • sites.js — sites derived from structures; the composers
  • levels.js · nerveLength.js — the two orthogonal axes
  • tracts.js — the long pathways and where each one crosses
  • compartments.js · course.js — where a lesion sits; how the illness unfolded
  • vascular.js · topography.js · substrate.js — vessel, place and the TISSUE a disease attacks

src/engine/

the generic solver — rarely touched

  • forward.js — site → expected signed findings
  • score.js — reward matches, penalise the rest
  • inverse.js — rank, minimal-set, attach the level
  • tracts.js — the course narrative and why-not-elsewhere
  • patterns.js — UMN/LMN synthesis; the functional flag
  • multifocal.js · space.js — one disease across two places

src/data/

what the lesion MEANS — remove it and the engine still localises

  • syndromes.js — a phonebook: emergent site → eponym
  • causes.js — the tempo-aware differential at each site
  • nextSteps.js — the four-tier workup
  • pathology/ · pathologyNextSteps.js — the workup narrowed to ONE disease
  • multifocal.js · multifocalNextSteps.js — the cross-site roster and its workups
